## Medieval Philosophy: Latin, Byzantine, Islamic, and Jewish Traditions (c. 750–1450)

### John of Damascus ☆ (c. 675–749)

**Position:** Byzantine Philosophy · Patristic Theology · Christian Aristotelianism · Philosophy of Religious Images

* **Name:** John of Damascus (Greek: *Iōannēs ho Damaskēnos*; Latin: *Ioannes Damascenus*).
* **Life:** Born in Damascus, then under Umayyad rule; Christian theologian, philosopher, and monk associated with the monastery of Mar Saba near Jerusalem. Died in the region of Jerusalem.
* **Contributions:** One of the most important systematic thinkers of late Greek Patristic philosophy. Organized earlier Christian theology through the use of Aristotelian logic and developed influential philosophical arguments concerning religious images, representation, and the relationship between divine and material reality.
* **Major Works:** *The Fount of Knowledge (Pēgē Gnōseōs)*, comprising *Philosophical Chapters (Dialectica)*, *On Heresies*, and *An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith*; *Three Treatises on the Divine Images*.

**The Fount of Knowledge**
* Systematic Theology, Philosophical Method, Logic, Doctrinal Classification.
* Can religious doctrines be organized into a systematic body of knowledge using philosophical concepts?
* How should logical analysis contribute to the clarification of theological disagreements?
* *The Fount of Knowledge*.

**Aristotelian Logic and Christian Theology**
* Aristotelian Categories, Predication, Definition, Substance, Accident.
* How can Aristotelian logical distinctions clarify the structure of theological propositions?
* What are the limits of applying concepts originally developed for finite beings to the divine?
* *Philosophical Chapters*.

**Substance, Nature, and Hypostasis**
* Substance (*Ousia*), Nature (*Physis*), Hypostasis (*Hypostasis*), Personhood.
* What distinguishes an individual existent from the nature it possesses?
* How can metaphysical distinctions concerning substance and person be applied to Trinitarian and Christological questions?
* *Philosophical Chapters*, *Exposition of the Orthodox Faith*.

**Divine Transcendence and Human Knowledge**
* Divine Transcendence, Negative Theology, Epistemic Limitations.
* To what extent can human concepts describe a reality understood as transcending ordinary experience?
* Why must affirmative theological claims be qualified by an acknowledgment of divine incomprehensibility?

**The Problem of Religious Images**
* Iconoclasm, Icon Veneration, Representation, Material Images.
* Can an invisible and transcendent God legitimately be represented through material images?
* What philosophical distinction exists between depicting something and claiming to reproduce its essence?
* *Three Treatises on the Divine Images*.

**The Incarnation and the Possibility of Representation**
* Incarnation, Visibility, Embodiment, Representation.
* How does the Christian doctrine of the Incarnation transform the theological status of visible representation?
* Why does the material embodiment of Christ become central to John's defense of icons?

**Veneration and Worship**
* Veneration (*Proskynēsis*), Worship (*Latreia*), Representation, Religious Practice.
* What distinguishes honoring an image from worshipping the divine?
* How can the distinction between an image and its prototype prevent religious representation from becoming idolatry?

**Image and Prototype**
* Image, Prototype, Similarity, Referential Representation.
* In what sense does an image direct attention toward something other than itself?
* What makes representation possible without requiring identity between an image and what it represents?

**Free Will and Moral Agency**
* Free Will, Rational Agency, Moral Responsibility, Divine Foreknowledge.
* How can moral responsibility be preserved within a theological system affirming divine foreknowledge?
* Why does the distinction between foreknowledge and causal determination matter?
* *An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith*.

**Philosophical Legacy**
* Aristotle, Porphyry, Greek Patristic Philosophy, Byzantine Theology, Icon Theory, Peter Lombard, Thomas Aquinas.
* John of Damascus provides an important transition between late ancient Greek philosophical theology and medieval Byzantine and Latin scholastic thought.

### Al-Kindi ☆ (c. 800–870)

**Position:** Early Islamic Philosophy · Arabic Aristotelianism · Neoplatonism · Metaphysics · Philosophy of Science

* **Name:** Abū Yūsuf Yaʿqūb ibn Isḥāq al-Kindī (Arabic: أبو يوسف يعقوب بن إسحاق الكندي).
* **Life:** Born around the beginning of the ninth century, probably in Kufa in the Abbasid Caliphate; Arab philosopher, mathematician, and scientist active principally in Baghdad. Associated with the translation of Greek philosophical and scientific works into Arabic.
* **Contributions:** Commonly regarded as the first major self-identified philosopher of the Arabic philosophical tradition. Played a foundational role in adapting Greek philosophical methods and concepts to an Islamic intellectual environment, particularly in metaphysics, psychology, cosmology, and the mathematical sciences.
* **Major Works:** *On First Philosophy (Fī al-Falsafa al-Ūlā)*, *On the Intellect (Fī al-ʿAql)*, *On the Quantity of Aristotle's Books*, *On Dispelling Sorrows*, *On Definitions and Descriptions of Things*, writings on mathematics, optics, and natural philosophy.

**The Introduction of Greek Philosophy into Arabic Thought**
* Greek–Arabic Translation Movement, Aristotle, Neoplatonism, Philosophical Transmission.
* How can philosophical concepts be transferred between intellectual traditions with different religious and linguistic foundations?
* What transformations occur when Aristotelian and Neoplatonic doctrines are incorporated into Arabic philosophy?

**The Legitimacy of Philosophy**
* Philosophy (*Falsafa*), Truth, Rational Inquiry, Intellectual Tradition.
* Why should philosophical truths be accepted regardless of the cultural or religious origins of their authors?
* How can rational inquiry contribute to the understanding of theological questions?
* *On First Philosophy*.

**First Philosophy and the First Cause**
* First Philosophy, Metaphysics, First Cause, Being, Truth.
* What is the proper subject matter of first philosophy?
* Why does al-Kindi connect the investigation of being and truth with the investigation of a first cause?
* *On First Philosophy*.

**Divine Unity**
* Divine Unity (*Tawḥīd*), True One, Simplicity, Multiplicity.
* What distinguishes absolute unity from the relative unity possessed by ordinary objects?
* Why does al-Kindi argue that the ultimate cause cannot possess the multiplicity characteristic of created things?

**The True One and Created Unity**
* Unity, Composition, Causal Dependence, Metaphysical Explanation.
* How do finite beings acquire their unity?
* Can the unity of composite entities be explained without appealing to a first source of unity?

**The Finitude of the World**
* Finite Magnitude, Finite Time, Eternity, Cosmological Arguments.
* Can the physical world have existed through an actually infinite past?
* How does al-Kindi challenge arguments for the eternity of the cosmos?
* *On First Philosophy*.

**Creation and Causation**
* Creation, First Cause, Temporal Origination, Causal Dependence.
* How does al-Kindi's account of creation differ from Aristotelian accounts of an eternal world?
* What philosophical problems arise when explaining the existence of the cosmos through a first cause?

**The Theory of Intellect**
* Intellect (*ʿAql*), Actuality, Potentiality, Knowledge.
* How does the human intellect move from potential understanding to actual knowledge?
* What role does an intellect that is always actual play in explaining human cognition?
* *On the Intellect*.

**The Soul and Human Cognition**
* Soul (*Nafs*), Perception, Intellectual Activity, Psychology.
* How should the relationship between sensory experience and intellectual understanding be explained?
* What distinguishes intellectual cognition from the operations of bodily senses?

**Mathematics and Philosophical Explanation**
* Mathematics, Quantity, Demonstration, Scientific Knowledge.
* Why does al-Kindi regard mathematical training as important for philosophical investigation?
* How can mathematical reasoning contribute to the explanation of natural phenomena?

**Natural Philosophy and Optics**
* Vision, Light, Geometry, Physical Explanation.
* How can geometrical analysis help explain visual perception?
* What distinguishes a mathematical account of perception from a physiological or metaphysical account?

**Ethics and the Regulation of Sorrow**
* Sorrow, Attachment, Rational Therapy, Human Happiness.
* Why does attachment to unstable worldly possessions generate suffering?
* How can philosophical reflection transform emotional responses to loss?
* *On Dispelling Sorrows*.

**The Relationship Between Philosophy and Religion**
* Rational Demonstration, Religious Truth, Metaphysics, Revelation.
* Can philosophy and religious belief converge on fundamental truths through different approaches?
* Where might philosophical demonstration and religious commitment generate competing explanations?

**Philosophical Legacy**
* Aristotle, Plotinus, Proclus, the Arabic Translation Movement, Al-Farabi, Avicenna, Medieval Islamic Philosophy, Latin Scholasticism.
* Al-Kindi establishes the foundations of a philosophical tradition later transformed by Al-Farabi, Avicenna, Averroes, and other major thinkers.

### John Scotus Eriugena ☆ (c. 815–877 CE)

**Position:** Early Medieval Philosophy · Carolingian Renaissance · Christian Neoplatonism · Metaphysics

* **Name:** John Scotus Eriugena (Latin: *Iohannes Scottus Eriugena*).
* **Life:** Irish philosopher, theologian, and translator; active at the court of Charles the Bald in the Carolingian Empire. His later life and death remain uncertain.
* **Contributions:** Developed one of the most ambitious philosophical systems of the early Middle Ages, integrating Greek Neoplatonism, Christian theology, dialectic, and theories of creation.
* **Major Works:** *Periphyseon (On the Division of Nature)*, *On Divine Predestination*, translations of Pseudo-Dionysius and Maximus the Confessor.

**The Division of Nature**
* Nature (*Natura*), the Four Divisions of Nature, Creation and Non-Creation, Being and Non-Being.
* How can the totality of reality be understood through a systematic division of nature?
* *Periphyseon* I–V.

**God, Creation, and Divine Transcendence**
* Negative Theology, Divine Manifestation, Creation *Ex Nihilo*, Theophany.
* How can God be the source of everything while transcending the categories of being?
* *Periphyseon* I–III.

**Procession and Return**
* Procession (*Processio*), Return (*Reditus*), Causal Order, Universal Restoration.
* Why does the metaphysical explanation of reality involve both emanation from and return to the first principle?
* *Periphyseon* II–V.

**Reason, Authority, and Philosophical Interpretation**
* Dialectic, Scriptural Interpretation, Reason and Authority.
* What should happen when an authoritative religious interpretation conflicts with rational argument?

**Philosophical Legacy**
* Augustine, Pseudo-Dionysius, Maximus the Confessor, Neoplatonism, Medieval Mystical Theology.

### Al-Farabi ☆ (c. 870–950 CE)

**Position:** Medieval Arabic Aristotelianism · Neoplatonic Influences · Logic · Political Philosophy · Philosophy of Religion

* **Name:** Abū Naṣr al-Fārābī (Arabic: أبو نصر الفارابي; Latin: *Alfarabius*).
* **Life:** Philosopher active principally in Baghdad and later in Syria. His precise birthplace and early biography remain uncertain.
* **Contributions:** An influential interpreter of Aristotle and Plato who developed original accounts of logic, knowledge, intellect, political organization, and the relationship between philosophical demonstration and religion.
* **Major Works:** *Enumeration of the Sciences*, *The Virtuous City*, *Political Regime*, *Attainment of Happiness*, *Book of Letters*, *On the Intellect*.

**Logic and the Classification of Knowledge**
* Aristotelian Logic, Demonstration, Dialectic, Rhetoric, the Classification of Sciences.
* How are different sciences distinguished by their objects and methods? Why does demonstrative reasoning possess a privileged epistemic status?
* *Enumeration of the Sciences*, *Book of Letters*.

**Intellect and Human Knowledge**
* Potential Intellect, Actual Intellect, Acquired Intellect, Active Intellect.
* How does human understanding develop from potentiality into actual intellectual knowledge?
* *On the Intellect*.

**Metaphysics and Cosmology**
* First Cause, Intellect, Emanation, Ontological Hierarchy.
* How can a multiplicity of beings arise from a single first principle?

**Political Philosophy and the Virtuous City**
* Virtuous City, Human Happiness, Philosophical Leadership, Political Community.
* What makes a political community genuinely virtuous? Why does political leadership require knowledge of the highest good?
* *The Virtuous City*, *Political Regime*.

**Philosophy, Religion, and Political Education**
* Demonstration, Imagination, Prophecy, Religion, Philosophical Truth.
* Can philosophical and religious discourse communicate the same truths through different methods?
* *Attainment of Happiness*.

**Philosophical Legacy**
* Plato, Aristotle, Avicenna, Maimonides, Medieval Political Philosophy.

### Avicenna ☆ (980–1037 CE)

**Position:** Medieval Arabic Philosophy · Avicennian Metaphysics · Aristotelianism · Neoplatonic Influences · Epistemology

* **Name:** Avicenna, also known as Ibn Sīnā (Arabic: ابن سينا; Latin: *Avicenna*).
* **Life:** Born near Bukhara in the Samanid realm, in present-day Uzbekistan; Persian philosopher, physician, and polymath. Died in Hamadan, present-day Iran.
* **Contributions:** Developed one of the most influential philosophical systems of the Middle Ages. His theories of existence, essence, necessity, causation, intellect, and the soul profoundly shaped subsequent Arabic and Latin philosophy.
* **Major Works:** *The Book of Healing (Kitāb al-Shifāʾ)*, *The Book of Salvation (Kitāb al-Najāt)*, *Pointers and Reminders (Al-Ishārāt wa-l-Tanbīhāt)*, *The Canon of Medicine*.

**Essence and Existence**
* Essence (*Māhiyya*), Existence (*Wujūd*), Quiddity, Ontological Dependence.
* What distinguishes what a thing is from the fact that it exists? Why is this distinction fundamental to metaphysics?
* *The Healing: Metaphysics*.

**Necessary and Contingent Existence**
* Necessary Existent (*Wājib al-Wujūd*), Possible Existent, Necessity, Contingency.
* Why must contingent beings ultimately depend on a necessary existent?
* *The Healing: Metaphysics*.

**The Proof of the Necessary Existent**
* Contingency Argument, First Principle, Causal Explanation.
* Can the existence of a necessary being be established through an analysis of existence itself?

**The Soul and the Floating Man**
* Self-Awareness, Immaterial Soul, Personal Identity, the Floating Man Thought Experiment.
* Can a person be aware of their own existence without any bodily sensation?
* *The Healing: On the Soul*.

**Intellect and Knowledge**
* Abstraction, Active Intellect, Intellectual Development, Universal Knowledge.
* How does the human intellect acquire universal concepts from experience?

**Causation and Emanation**
* Efficient Causation, Necessity, Emanation, Cosmic Intellects.
* How does a necessary first principle account for a contingent and differentiated universe?

**Philosophical Legacy**
* Al-Farabi, Al-Ghazali, Averroes, Albert the Great, Thomas Aquinas, Duns Scotus, Latin Scholastic Metaphysics.

### Michael Psellos ☆ (1018–c. 1078)

**Position:** Byzantine Philosophy · Byzantine Platonism · Neoplatonism · Aristotelian Logic · Intellectual History

* **Name:** Michael Psellos (Greek: *Michaēl Psellos*; born Constantine Psellos).
* **Life:** Born in Constantinople, Byzantine Empire; philosopher, historian, rhetorician, teacher, and imperial official. His precise date of death remains uncertain, with approximately 1078 commonly used.
* **Contributions:** One of the major intellectual figures of medieval Byzantium. Played an important role in the teaching and interpretation of ancient Greek philosophy, particularly Plato, Aristotle, and the Neoplatonists, and contributed to the continuity of philosophical education in the Greek-speaking world.
* **Major Works:** *Chronographia*, *De Omnifaria Doctrina (On All Aspects of Knowledge)*, philosophical and theological treatises, commentaries and paraphrases on Aristotelian logic, writings on Platonic philosophy and the human soul.

**The Revival of Philosophical Education**
* Byzantine Intellectual Culture, Classical Learning, Philosophical Curriculum.
* How were ancient Greek philosophical traditions preserved and transformed within medieval Byzantium?
* What role did philosophical education play in political, religious, and intellectual life?

**The Relationship Between Plato and Aristotle**
* Platonism, Aristotelianism, Metaphysics, Logic.
* How can the philosophical systems of Plato and Aristotle be studied within a shared educational tradition?
* Why does Psellos display a marked preference for Platonic and Neoplatonic philosophy despite his extensive engagement with Aristotle?

**The Study of Aristotelian Logic**
* Aristotelian Logic, Categories, Predication, Syllogistic Reasoning.
* How can Aristotelian logical techniques contribute to the clarification of philosophical and theological problems?
* *Commentaries and Paraphrases on the Organon*.

**Platonism and Neoplatonic Metaphysics**
* Forms, Intelligible Reality, Hierarchy of Being, Causation.
* How should the relationship between sensible reality and intelligible principles be understood?
* To what extent can Neoplatonic metaphysics be reconciled with Byzantine Christian theology?

**De Omnifaria Doctrina**
* Philosophical Encyclopedia, Metaphysics, Psychology, Natural Philosophy.
* How can diverse philosophical questions be organized into a concise educational system?
* What does the structure of Psellos's philosophical compendium reveal about Byzantine intellectual priorities?
* *De Omnifaria Doctrina*.

**Substance and Self-Existence**
* Substance (*Ousia*), Self-Existence (*Authyparktos*), Ontological Dependence.
* What distinguishes entities that exist through themselves from those dependent on other things?
* How are questions of substance and dependence connected to metaphysical explanation?

**The Soul and the Body**
* Soul (*Psychē*), Embodiment, Human Nature, Psychological Unity.
* How should the unity of the human person be explained?
* Can the soul be understood as distinct from the body without rendering their relationship unintelligible?

**The Problem of Evil**
* Evil, Privation, Divine Goodness, Metaphysical Order.
* How can evil be explained within a metaphysical system organized around the goodness of ultimate reality?

**Philosophy and Christian Theology**
* Christian Theology, Pagan Philosophy, Intellectual Authority.
* Under what conditions can philosophical concepts developed outside Christianity be incorporated into Christian thought?
* How does the interpretation of ancient philosophy change within a theological setting?

**Philosophy, History, and Political Life**
* Political Judgment, Historical Narrative, Court Culture, Rhetoric.
* How do philosophical education and rhetorical skill contribute to political authority?
* What can Psellos's historical writing reveal about the interaction between intellectual life and imperial politics?
* *Chronographia*.

**Philosophical Legacy**
* Plato, Aristotle, Plotinus, Proclus, John Italos, Byzantine Platonism, Medieval Greek Philosophy, Renaissance Reception of Greek Learning.
* Psellos illustrates why the continuity of ancient Greek philosophy cannot be adequately understood through Latin and Arabic traditions alone.

### Anselm of Canterbury ☆ (1033–1109 CE)

**Position:** Early Scholastic Philosophy · Augustinianism · Philosophical Theology · Philosophy of Religion

* **Name:** Anselm of Canterbury (Latin: *Anselmus Cantuariensis*; /ˈænsɛlm/).
* **Life:** Born in Aosta; joined the Benedictine community at Bec in Normandy and later became Archbishop of Canterbury. Died in Canterbury.
* **Contributions:** Developed influential arguments for God's existence, divine attributes, truth, freedom, moral responsibility, and the relationship between faith and rational understanding.
* **Major Works:** *Monologion*, *Proslogion*, *On Truth*, *On Freedom of Choice*, *On the Fall of the Devil*, *Why God Became Man*, *On the Agreement of Foreknowledge, Predestination, and Grace with Free Choice*.

**Faith Seeking Understanding**
* Faith Seeking Understanding (*Fides Quaerens Intellectum*), Rational Inquiry, Philosophical Theology.
* How can reason investigate truths initially accepted through faith?
* *Proslogion*.

**The Ontological Argument**
* That Than Which Nothing Greater Can Be Thought, Necessary Existence, Existence in the Understanding and in Reality.
* Can the existence of God be established from the concept of God alone?
* *Proslogion* II–III.

**Divine Attributes and Perfection**
* Divine Simplicity, Eternity, Omnipotence, Goodness, Necessity.
* What properties must belong to a supremely perfect being?
* *Monologion*, *Proslogion*.

**Truth and Rectitude**
* Truth (*Veritas*), Rectitude (*Rectitudo*), Judgment, Will.
* What unifies truth in thought, language, and action?
* *On Truth*.

**Free Will and Moral Responsibility**
* Freedom of Choice, Rectitude of Will, Moral Agency, Sin.
* Is freedom the ability to choose between good and evil, or the capacity to preserve the rightness of one's will?
* *On Freedom of Choice*.

**Divine Foreknowledge and Human Freedom**
* Necessity, Contingency, Foreknowledge, Predestination.
* Can a future human action remain free if God already knows it?

**The Ontological Argument and Its Critics**
* Gaunilo's Objection, Kant's Criticism, Modern Modal Ontological Arguments.
* Does conceptual analysis establish real existence?

### Solomon Ibn Gabirol ☆ (c. 1021–1058 CE)

* **Major Works:** *Fountain of Life (Fons Vitae / Yanbuʿ al-Ḥayāh)*, *The Improvement of the Moral Qualities*.

**Universal Hylomorphism**

* Are all created substances, including immaterial beings, composed of matter and form?
* *Fons Vitae*.

**The First Principle and Divine Will**
* First Cause, Divine Will, Emanation, Metaphysical Unity.
* How can an absolutely unified source produce a differentiated world?

**Metaphysical Hierarchy**
* Ontological Levels, Participation, Spiritual and Material Reality.
* What explains the relationship between different grades of existence?

**Philosophical Legacy**
* Neoplatonism, Medieval Jewish Philosophy, Franciscan Scholasticism, Bonaventure, Thomas Aquinas.

### Al-Ghazali ☆ (c. 1058–1111 CE)

**Position:** Medieval Islamic Philosophical Theology · Criticism of Aristotelianism · Epistemology · Philosophy of Religion

* **Name:** Abū Ḥāmid al-Ghazālī (Arabic: أبو حامد الغزالي; Latin: *Algazel*).
* **Life:** Born in Tus, Khurasan, in present-day Iran; philosopher, theologian, and scholar associated with Baghdad and other centers of learning.
* **Contributions:** Developed major critiques of Avicennian metaphysics and philosophical accounts of causation, eternity, and divine knowledge. His works also contributed to the transmission of Arabic philosophical doctrines into Latin scholasticism.
* **Major Works:** *The Incoherence of the Philosophers*, *The Aims of the Philosophers*, *Deliverance from Error*, *The Revival of the Religious Sciences*.

**Critique of Philosophical Necessity**
* Causation, Necessity, Contingency, Divine Power.
* Are causal connections necessarily established by the natures of things?
* *The Incoherence of the Philosophers*, Discussion 17.

**Causation and Occasionalism**
* Occasionalism, Divine Agency, Natural Regularity, Miracles.
* Can natural regularities be explained without necessary causal connections? Must al-Ghazali's argument be interpreted as a commitment to universal occasionalism?

**Criticism of the Eternity of the World**
* Temporal Creation, Cosmic Eternity, Divine Will.
* Can philosophical arguments establish that the universe has existed eternally?

**Knowledge, Certainty, and Skeptical Inquiry**
* Certainty, Sense Perception, Rational Knowledge, Intellectual Doubt.
* How can a thinker move from doubt toward justified certainty?
* *Deliverance from Error*.

**Philosophical Legacy**
* Avicenna, Averroes, Causation Debates, Medieval Latin Reception of Arabic Philosophy.

### Peter Abelard ☆ (1079–1142 CE)

**Position:** Twelfth-Century Scholasticism · Dialectic · Logic · Philosophy of Language · Ethics

* **Name:** Peter Abelard (Latin: *Petrus Abaelardus*; /ˈæbəlɑːrd/).
* **Life:** Born near Nantes in Brittany; taught in Paris and other French schools. Later entered monastic life. Died at the Priory of Saint-Marcel.
* **Contributions:** A leading figure of twelfth-century scholastic philosophy who made original contributions to logic, universals, language, philosophical methodology, and intention-based ethics.
* **Major Works:** *Dialectica*, *Logica Ingredientibus*, *Sic et Non*, *Ethics (Scito te Ipsum)*, *Theologia Summi Boni*, *Historia Calamitatum*.

**The Problem of Universals**
* Universals, Nominalism, Predication, Common Natures, Linguistic Signification.
* What makes a universal term applicable to many distinct individuals?
* *Logica Ingredientibus*.

**Logic and the Theory of Language**
* Propositions, Meaning, Predication, Entailment, Semantic Analysis.
* How can logic distinguish the structure of language from the structure of reality?
* *Dialectica*.

**Dialectic and the Scholastic Method**
* Contradictory Authorities, Questioning, Logical Resolution.
* How can apparently contradictory authoritative statements be reconciled through disciplined reasoning?
* *Sic et Non*.

**Ethics of Intention**
* Intention, Consent, Sin, Moral Responsibility, Moral Evaluation.
* Does the morality of an action depend principally on its consequences, its external performance, or the agent's intention?
* *Ethics*.

**Reason and Theology**
* Rational Explanation, Divine Attributes, Philosophical Theology.
* How far can philosophical argument clarify religious doctrine?

**Philosophical Legacy**
* Boethius, Early Scholastic Logic, Héloïse, Peter Lombard, Medieval Nominalism.

### Averroes ☆ (1126–1198 CE)

**Position:** Medieval Arabic Aristotelianism · Aristotelian Commentary · Metaphysics · Philosophy of Mind

* **Name:** Averroes, also known as Ibn Rushd (Arabic: ابن رشد; Latin: *Averroes*).
