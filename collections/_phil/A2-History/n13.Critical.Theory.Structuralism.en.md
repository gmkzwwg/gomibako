## Critical Theory, Structuralism, and Poststructuralism (c. 1920–2000)

### Sigmund Freud ☆ (1856–1939)

**Position:** Psychoanalysis · Depth Psychology · Theory of the Unconscious · Philosophy of Culture · Psychoanalytic Foundations of Critical Theory

* **Name:** Sigmund Freud (/frɔɪd/).
* **Life:** Born in Freiberg, Moravia, Austrian Empire (present-day Příbor, Czech Republic); Austrian neurologist and founder of psychoanalysis. Worked principally in Vienna and emigrated to London in 1938 to escape Nazi persecution. Died in London.
* **Contributions:** Developed psychoanalysis as a theory of mental life and a method of psychological interpretation, emphasizing unconscious processes, psychic conflict, repression, desire, and the relationship between individual psychology and civilization. His theories exerted a profound influence on twentieth-century philosophy, literature, cultural criticism, and social theory, although many of their empirical claims remain contested.
* **Major Works:** *The Interpretation of Dreams*, *The Psychopathology of Everyday Life*, *Three Essays on the Theory of Sexuality*, *Introductory Lectures on Psycho-Analysis*, *Beyond the Pleasure Principle*, *The Ego and the Id*, *The Future of an Illusion*, *Civilization and Its Discontents*, *Totem and Taboo*, *Moses and Monotheism*.

**The Discovery of the Dynamic Unconscious**
* Unconscious (*Unbewusst*), Psychic Conflict, Unconscious Motivation.
* To what extent can mental processes influence behavior without becoming conscious?
* What distinguishes Freud's dynamic unconscious from the ordinary claim that some information is outside awareness?

**The Topographical Model of the Mind**
* Conscious, Preconscious, Unconscious, Mental Systems.
* How does Freud distinguish conscious mental contents from material that is accessible or actively excluded from awareness?
* What explanatory role does the topographical model play in his early theory of the mind?
* *The Interpretation of Dreams*.

**The Structural Model of the Mind**
* Id (*Es*), Ego (*Ich*), Superego (*Über-Ich*).
* What psychological functions are attributed to the id, ego, and superego?
* How does the later structural model modify the earlier conscious–preconscious–unconscious distinction?
* *The Ego and the Id*.

**The Id**
* Id, Drives, Unconscious Motivation, Pleasure Principle.
* How does Freud characterize the mental processes associated with instinctual demands?
* Why does the id resist the constraints of rational and social organization?

**The Ego**
* Ego, Reality Principle, Mediation, Psychological Defense.
* How does the ego negotiate conflicts among instinctual demands, external reality, and internalized norms?
* Why does Freud reject the assumption that the ego is entirely conscious?

**The Superego**
* Superego, Internalized Authority, Guilt, Moral Regulation.
* How do social prohibitions and parental authority contribute to the formation of internal psychological demands?

**Repression**
* Repression (*Verdrängung*), Unconscious Conflict, Defense Mechanisms.
* How does Freud explain the exclusion of psychologically threatening material from consciousness?
* What methodological problems arise when repression is used to explain both the presence and absence of remembered experiences?

**Resistance**
* Resistance (*Widerstand*), Psychological Defense, Psychoanalytic Interpretation.
* Why might individuals resist recognizing certain motives or conflicts?
* How can resistance be distinguished from ordinary disagreement with an interpretation?

**Transference**
* Transference (*Übertragung*), Interpersonal Patterns, Psychoanalytic Relationship.
* How can patterns of expectation and emotion become reorganized within a therapeutic relationship?
* What role does transference play in Freud's account of psychological change?

**Free Association**
* Free Association, Interpretation, Psychological Meaning.
* How does the method of free association attempt to investigate unconscious associations?
* What assumptions are required to infer hidden psychological processes from verbal associations?

**Dreams and Wish Fulfilment**
* Dreams, Wish Fulfilment, Unconscious Desire.
* Why does Freud treat dreams as meaningful psychological productions rather than random mental events?
* Can dream interpretation yield reliable knowledge of unconscious motivations?
* *The Interpretation of Dreams*.

**Manifest and Latent Dream Content**
* Manifest Content, Latent Dream Thoughts, Interpretation.
* What distinguishes the remembered content of a dream from the meanings Freud attributes to its formation?

**Dream-Work**
* Dream-Work (*Traumarbeit*), Condensation, Displacement, Representation.
* How do mechanisms such as condensation and displacement transform latent thoughts into manifest dream content?
* *The Interpretation of Dreams*.

**Psychopathology of Everyday Life**
* Slips of the Tongue, Forgetting, Everyday Errors, Psychological Motivation.
* Can apparently trivial mistakes reveal underlying psychological conflicts?
* What is the risk of interpreting every error as evidence of unconscious intention?
* *The Psychopathology of Everyday Life*.

**The Theory of Drives**
* Drives (*Triebe*), Psychic Energy, Motivation, Instinctual Demands.
* How do bodily demands become represented in mental life?
* What distinguishes Freud's drive theory from a simple model of biological reflexes?

**Sexuality and Psychosexual Development**
* Libido, Psychosexual Development, Childhood Sexuality.
* Why does Freud extend the concept of sexuality beyond adult reproductive behavior?
* What are the explanatory assumptions and empirical difficulties of his developmental theory?
* *Three Essays on the Theory of Sexuality*.

**The Oedipus Complex**
* Oedipus Complex, Identification, Desire, Family Relations.
* How does Freud attempt to explain the development of desire, authority, and identification within familial relationships?
* To what extent do his claims depend on historically specific assumptions about gender and family structure?

**The Pleasure Principle and Reality Principle**
* Pleasure Principle, Reality Principle, Desire, Adaptation.
* How does Freud explain the conflict between immediate gratification and the demands of external reality?

**Repetition Compulsion**
* Repetition Compulsion, Psychological Repetition, Trauma.
* Why might individuals repeat experiences that do not appear to produce pleasure?
* *Beyond the Pleasure Principle*.

**Life and Death Drives**
* Eros, Death Drive (*Todestrieb*), Aggression, Psychic Conflict.
* Why does Freud introduce a speculative opposition between life-preserving and destructive tendencies?
* What are the conceptual and evidential limitations of this theory?
* *Beyond the Pleasure Principle*.

**Sublimation**
* Sublimation, Cultural Production, Instinctual Transformation.
* How can drives be redirected into artistic, intellectual, and socially valued activities?

**Civilization and Repression**
* Civilization, Social Norms, Instinctual Restraint, Discontent.
* Why does Freud regard civilized life as both necessary for human cooperation and a source of psychological conflict?
* *Civilization and Its Discontents*.

**Religion and Wish Fulfilment**
* Religious Belief, Illusion, Dependence, Psychological Security.
* How does Freud interpret religious belief through psychological needs and desires?
* Why must a psychological explanation of belief be distinguished from a demonstration that the belief is false?
* *The Future of an Illusion*.

**The Psychoanalytic Theory of Culture**
* Culture, Authority, Desire, Guilt, Collective Life.
* How can psychological conflicts contribute to the organization of social institutions?
* What changes when theories of individual psychology are extended to social and political explanation?

**Psychoanalysis and the Frankfurt School**
* Freudo-Marxism, Social Repression, Authoritarianism, Desire.
* How did Frankfurt School thinkers use psychoanalysis to explain the psychological reproduction of political authority?
* How do Horkheimer, Adorno, Marcuse, and Fromm transform Freud's original concepts?

**Freud and Structural Psychoanalysis**
* Structural Psychoanalysis, Language, Desire, the Unconscious.
* How does Lacan reinterpret Freud through linguistic and structural concepts?
* What distinguishes Lacan's account of the unconscious from Freud's original psychological explanations?

**Freud and the Hermeneutics of Suspicion**
* Interpretation, Hidden Motivation, Suspicion, Self-Knowledge.
* How does psychoanalysis challenge the assumption that individuals possess transparent knowledge of their own motives?
* Why does Ricoeur place Freud alongside Marx and Nietzsche within the hermeneutics of suspicion?

**The Scientific Status of Psychoanalysis**
* Scientific Explanation, Testability, Interpretation, Clinical Evidence.
* Which psychoanalytic claims are empirically testable?
* How should the theoretical influence of psychoanalysis be distinguished from the scientific confirmation of its clinical and psychological claims?

**Philosophical Legacy**
* Nietzsche, Schopenhauer, Lacan, Fromm, Marcuse, Adorno, Ricoeur, Deleuze, Feminist Psychoanalytic Theory, Critical Theory, Poststructuralism.
* Freud is indispensable to the intellectual foundations of twentieth-century theories of subjectivity and culture, but the philosophical significance of psychoanalysis should not be confused with comprehensive empirical validation of Freudian psychology.

### Ferdinand de Saussure ☆ (1857–1913)

**Position:** Structural Linguistics · Semiotics · Foundations of Structuralism · Philosophy of Language

* **Name:** Ferdinand de Saussure (/soʊˈsjʊər/).
* **Life:** Born in Geneva, Switzerland; linguist who studied in Leipzig and Berlin and taught in Paris and Geneva. Died in Vufflens-le-Château, Switzerland.
* **Contributions:** A foundational figure of structural linguistics whose account of language as a system of differences became indispensable to twentieth-century structuralism and poststructuralism.
* **Major Works:** *Course in General Linguistics* (published posthumously in 1916, edited from lecture notes by Charles Bally and Albert Sechehaye, with the collaboration of Albert Riedlinger), *Memoir on the Primitive System of Vowels in Indo-European Languages*.

**The Linguistic Sign**
* Linguistic Sign, Signifier (*Signifiant*), Signified (*Signifié*), Signification.
* How does the relationship between a signifier and a signified constitute a linguistic sign?
* *Course in General Linguistics*.

**The Arbitrariness of the Sign**
* Arbitrariness, Convention, Signification, Linguistic Systems.
* Why is the connection between a linguistic signifier and its signified generally conventional rather than naturally necessary?

**Langue and Parole**
* *Langue*, *Parole*, Linguistic System, Individual Speech.
* How should a shared linguistic system be distinguished from its individual instances of use?

**Synchronic and Diachronic Linguistics**
* Synchronic Analysis, Diachronic Analysis, Linguistic Change.
* Why can the structure of a language at a given time be investigated independently of its historical development?

**Difference and Linguistic Value**
* Differential Relations, Linguistic Value (*Valeur*), Structural Identity.
* How can a linguistic element acquire its identity through relations of difference rather than intrinsic properties?

**Semiology and the General Study of Signs**
* Semiology (*Sémiologie*), Sign Systems, Social Conventions.
* Can the principles of linguistic analysis be extended to other systems of signification?

**Philosophical Legacy**
* Roman Jakobson, Claude Lévi-Strauss, Roland Barthes, Jacques Lacan, Jacques Derrida, Structuralism, Semiotics.

### Max Weber ☆ (1864–1920)

**Position:** Interpretive Social Science · Philosophy of Social Science · Political Sociology · Theory of Rationalization · Social and Political Theory

* **Name:** Maximilian Carl Emil Weber (/ˈveɪbər/).
* **Life:** Born in Erfurt, Kingdom of Prussia; German sociologist, historian, legal scholar, and political thinker who taught at Freiburg, Heidelberg, Vienna, and Munich. Died in Munich.
* **Contributions:** One of the foundational theorists of modern social science. Developed influential approaches to social action, interpretive understanding, rationalization, bureaucracy, legitimate authority, religion, scientific objectivity, and political responsibility. His work became a major intellectual foundation for later Critical Theory.
* **Major Works:** *The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism*, *Economy and Society* (published posthumously in edited form), *The Objectivity of the Sociological and Social-Political Knowledge*, *Science as a Vocation*, *Politics as a Vocation*, *The Methodology of the Social Sciences* (collected essays), *The Religion of China*, *The Religion of India*, *Ancient Judaism*.

**The Foundations of Interpretive Sociology**
* Interpretive Sociology, Social Action, Meaning, Causal Explanation.
* What makes an action genuinely social rather than merely individual behavior?
* How can social science combine interpretation of subjective meaning with causal explanation?
* *Economy and Society*.

**Verstehen**
* Understanding (*Verstehen*), Subjective Meaning, Interpretation.
* What does it mean to understand an action from the standpoint of the meaning attributed to it by the actor?
* Why must interpretive understanding be distinguished from simply sympathizing with an individual's feelings?

**Social Action**
* Social Action (*Soziales Handeln*), Subjective Meaning, Orientation to Others.
* How do individuals orient their actions toward the actual or anticipated conduct of others?
* How does this orientation distinguish social action from behavior lacking social meaning?

**The Four Types of Social Action**
* Instrumentally Rational (*Zweckrational*), Value-Rational (*Wertrational*), Affectual, Traditional Action.
* How do different motivations organize social action?
* Can apparently similar behaviors be explained through different forms of rationality or commitment?
* *Economy and Society*.

**Ideal Types**
* Ideal Types (*Idealtypen*), Analytical Construction, Conceptual Comparison.
* Why does Weber construct ideal types that deliberately emphasize selected features of empirical phenomena?
* How can ideal types contribute to explanation without claiming to provide exact descriptions of reality?

**Value-Relevance and Value Neutrality**
* Value-Relevance (*Wertbeziehung*), Value Neutrality (*Wertfreiheit*), Objectivity.
* How do values influence which social phenomena researchers regard as significant?
* How can inquiry remain scientifically disciplined despite the role of values in selecting research problems?
* *The Objectivity of the Sociological and Social-Political Knowledge*.

**The Limits of Scientific Knowledge**
* Scientific Objectivity, Normative Judgment, Empirical Explanation.
* Can empirical science determine which ultimate values individuals ought to adopt?
* Why does Weber distinguish the scientific investigation of consequences from the philosophical or political choice of ends?
* *Science as a Vocation*.

**Rationalization**
* Rationalization (*Rationalisierung*), Calculation, Predictability, Systematic Organization.
* How does modern society increasingly organize action through calculation, rules, and procedural efficiency?
* Why does rationalization transform both institutions and individual experience?

**Formal and Substantive Rationality**
* Formal Rationality, Substantive Rationality, Economic Calculation.
* How do calculability and procedural consistency differ from evaluation according to substantive ethical or political values?
* Why can formally rational systems produce substantively undesirable outcomes?

**Disenchantment**
* Disenchantment (*Entzauberung*), Intellectualization, Modernity.
* What does Weber mean by the disenchantment of the world?
* Why should disenchantment not be reduced simply to the disappearance of religion?

**The Protestant Ethic**
* Protestant Ethic, Ascetic Protestantism, Capitalist Spirit, Religious Motivation.
* How did particular religious ethical orientations contribute to the development of disciplined economic conduct?
* Why is Weber's argument about an elective affinity between religion and capitalism more complex than the claim that Protestantism simply caused capitalism?
* *The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism*.

**The Spirit of Capitalism**
* Capitalist Spirit, Vocational Discipline, Rational Profit-Seeking.
* How does the pursuit of profit become organized as a systematic ethical and practical orientation?
* What distinguishes modern rational capitalism from ordinary acquisitiveness?

**The Iron Cage**
* Iron Cage (*Stahlhartes Gehäuse*), Institutional Constraint, Rationalized Organization.
* How can institutions originally supported by meaningful ethical commitments become systems experienced as impersonal constraints?
* Why does rationalization threaten certain forms of autonomy and value-oriented action?

**Bureaucracy**
* Bureaucracy, Hierarchy, Specialization, Formal Rules, Administrative Efficiency.
* Why does bureaucratic organization become central to modern government and large institutions?
* How can the same organizational characteristics produce both efficiency and domination?
* *Economy and Society*.

**Power and Domination**
* Power (*Macht*), Domination (*Herrschaft*), Authority, Legitimacy.
* How does Weber distinguish the capacity to impose one's will from a structured relationship of obedience?
* Why is legitimacy important to the stability of domination?

**The Three Pure Types of Legitimate Domination**
* Traditional Authority, Charismatic Authority, Legal-Rational Authority.
* What explains obedience to authority based on tradition, personal charisma, or formally established rules?
* How do these ideal types illuminate modern political institutions?
* *Economy and Society*.

**Charismatic Authority**
* Charisma, Personal Authority, Recognition, Social Transformation.
* Why can extraordinary personal authority disrupt established institutional systems?
* What makes charismatic domination unstable over time?

**The Routinization of Charisma**
* Routinization, Institutionalization, Succession, Administrative Order.
* How can authority based on personal recognition become a stable institutional arrangement?
* What changes when charismatic movements develop offices, rules, and bureaucratic structures?

**Class, Status, and Party**
* Class, Status Groups, Parties, Social Stratification.
* Why does Weber distinguish economic class, social status, and political organization?
* How does his multidimensional approach differ from theories emphasizing economic class alone?
* *Economy and Society*.

**The State and the Monopoly of Legitimate Physical Force**
* State, Legitimacy, Territorial Authority, Coercion.
* How does Weber define the modern state through its claim to the monopoly of the legitimate use of physical force within a territory?
* Why must this definition be understood as an analytical characterization of political organization rather than an endorsement of every state action?
* *Politics as a Vocation*.

**Politics as a Vocation**
* Political Leadership, Responsibility, Power, Ethical Commitment.
* What distinguishes political leadership from administrative expertise or purely moral advocacy?
* Why must political actors confront the unpredictable consequences of their decisions?
* *Politics as a Vocation*.

**The Ethics of Conviction**
* Ethics of Conviction (*Gesinnungsethik*), Principle, Moral Commitment.
* How does action guided by principled conviction differ from action evaluated through foreseeable consequences?

**The Ethics of Responsibility**
* Ethics of Responsibility (*Verantwortungsethik*), Consequences, Accountability.
* Why must political actors take responsibility for the likely effects of their decisions?
* How can responsibility for consequences coexist with commitment to ethical principles?

**The Tension Between Conviction and Responsibility**
* Political Ethics, Practical Judgment, Conflicting Values.
* Can the ethics of conviction and the ethics of responsibility be integrated rather than treated as mutually exclusive alternatives?

**The Disenchantment of Science**
* Scientific Vocation, Specialization, Intellectual Progress.
* Why does scientific progress generate increasingly specialized knowledge without resolving ultimate questions of meaning?
* *Science as a Vocation*.

**The Conflict of Value Spheres**
* Value Pluralism, Ethical Conflict, Modernity.
* Why can religious, economic, political, aesthetic, and scientific values come into conflict without a single rational principle resolving every disagreement?

**The Relationship Between Knowledge and Political Judgment**
* Empirical Inquiry, Political Decision-Making, Normative Commitments.
* What can scientific research establish about public policy?
* Why must the selection of political goals remain distinguishable from the technical analysis of methods?

**Weber and Marx**
* Capitalism, Social Class, Historical Explanation, Institutional Power.
* How does Weber's multidimensional account of class, status, and political organization differ from Marx's analysis of capitalist relations of production?
* Can rationalization and class relations provide complementary explanations of modern institutions?

**Weber and Nietzsche**
* Modernity, Value Conflict, Rationalization, Power.
* How does Nietzsche's critique of values illuminate Weber's diagnosis of modern disenchantment?
* How does Weber transform philosophical concerns about value and power into social-scientific analysis?

**Weber and the Frankfurt School**
* Rationalization, Instrumental Reason, Bureaucracy, Domination.
* How do Horkheimer, Adorno, and Marcuse transform Weber's account of rationalization into a critique of modern social domination?
* Why does Habermas distinguish communicative rationality from the instrumental forms emphasized in classical theories of rationalization?

**Weber and Foucault**
* Bureaucratic Rationality, Governmentality, Institutional Discipline.
* How does Weber's analysis of rationalized authority compare with Foucault's account of disciplinary power and governmentality?
* Can modern power be understood exclusively through formal political authority, or must it also be investigated through dispersed institutional practices?

**Philosophical Legacy**
* Kant, Nietzsche, Marx, Neo-Kantianism, Karl Mannheim, Horkheimer, Adorno, Marcuse, Habermas, Foucault, Political Sociology, Philosophy of Social Science.
* Weber supplies a central theoretical connection between nineteenth-century concerns about modern rationality and twentieth-century Critical Theory, political sociology, and the philosophy of social institutions.

### Georg Lukács ☆ (1885–1971)

* **Name:** Georg Lukács (Hungarian: György Lukács).
* **Life:** Born in Budapest, Austria-Hungary; Hungarian philosopher and literary critic. Died in Budapest.
* **Contributions:** Developed influential theories of reification, class consciousness, historical totality, and the relationship between capitalist social structures and forms of thought.
* **Major Works:** *The Theory of the Novel*, *History and Class Consciousness*, *The Young Hegel*, *The Destruction of Reason*, *The Ontology of Social Being* (posthumous).

**Reification**
* Reification (*Verdinglichung*), Commodity Form, Objectification, Social Relations.
* How do historically specific social relationships come to appear as relations between independent things?
* *History and Class Consciousness*.

**Class Consciousness**
* Class Consciousness, Social Position, Historical Agency.
* How does a social class become capable of understanding and transforming its historical circumstances?

**Totality and Social Explanation**
* Totality, Mediation, Historical Relations, Social Structures.
* Why does Lukács argue that social phenomena cannot be adequately understood in isolation?

**The Subject–Object of History**
* Historical Agency, Class, Dialectics, Social Transformation.
* In what sense can a collective social agent become both an object and a subject of historical knowledge?

**Criticism of Positivism**
* Positivism, Historical Materialism, Dialectical Method.
* Why might methods modeled on the natural sciences obscure the historical constitution of social reality?

**Philosophical Legacy**
* Hegel, Marx, Frankfurt School, Sartre, Western Marxism, Critical Theory.

### Antonio Gramsci ☆ (1891–1937)

* **Name:** Antonio Francesco Gramsci (/ˈɡrɑːmʃi/).

* **Major Works:** *Prison Notebooks*, *Letters from Prison*, earlier political and journalistic writings.

**Cultural Hegemony**
* Cultural Hegemony (*Egemonia Culturale*), Consent, Leadership, Social Power.
* How can dominant groups maintain social authority through cultural and intellectual leadership rather than coercion alone?
* *Prison Notebooks*.

**Civil Society and Political Society**
* Civil Society, Political Society, State, Coercion and Consent.
* How do institutions outside formal government contribute to the maintenance of political power?

**Organic Intellectuals**
* Organic Intellectuals, Traditional Intellectuals, Class Formation.
* How do intellectuals organize and articulate the experiences and interests of social groups?

**The Philosophy of Praxis**
* Philosophy of Praxis, Historical Materialism, Human Agency.
* How should Marxist philosophy understand the reciprocal relationship between thought and historical practice?

**War of Position and War of Manoeuvre**
* War of Position, War of Manoeuvre, Political Strategy, Institutional Power.
* Why might transforming social institutions require a prolonged struggle over legitimacy and cultural leadership?

**Common Sense and Good Sense**
* Common Sense (*Senso Comune*), Good Sense (*Buon Senso*), Everyday Consciousness.
* How can critical political understanding emerge from ordinary social beliefs?

**Philosophical Legacy**
* Marx, Lenin, Western Marxism, Cultural Studies, Political Theory, Postcolonial Studies.

**The Philosophy of History**
* Historical Materialism, Progress, Historical Rupture, Remembrance.

* *On the Concept of History*.

**Jetztzeit and Historical Time**
* *Jetztzeit* (Now-Time), Historical Constellation, Revolutionary Possibility.
* How can the present transform the meaning and significance of the past?

**The Angel of History**
* Angel of History, Catastrophe, Progress, Historical Memory.

**Allegory and Cultural Interpretation**

**Commodity Culture and Urban Experience**

* *The Arcades Project*.

**Translation and the Life of Language**
* Translation, Linguistic Difference, Translatability, Pure Language.

* *The Task of the Translator*.

**Philosophical Legacy**

**Traditional and Critical Theory**

* *Traditional and Critical Theory*.

**The Interdisciplinary Program of Critical Theory**
* Philosophy, Political Economy, Sociology, Psychology.
* Why must the analysis of domination integrate philosophical criticism with empirical social research?

**Instrumental Reason**
* Instrumental Rationality, Means–End Calculation, Technical Control.
* What happens when rationality becomes primarily a means of selecting efficient instruments for predetermined ends?
* *Eclipse of Reason*.

**Objective and Subjective Reason**
* Objective Reason, Subjective Reason, Rational Norms.
* Can reason evaluate the worth of ends rather than merely calculate effective means?

**The Dialectic of Enlightenment**
* Enlightenment, Myth, Domination, Rationalization.
* How can processes intended to liberate human beings generate new forms of domination?
* *Dialectic of Enlightenment*.

**Critique and Emancipation**
* Social Critique, Historical Possibility, Human Emancipation.
* What grounds a critique of society when existing norms and institutions are themselves part of the problem?

**Philosophical Legacy**

### Erich Fromm (1900–1980)
