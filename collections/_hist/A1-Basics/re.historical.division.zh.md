# World History Periodization

This note is both a reusable prompt and an applied reference for dividing world history into meaningful periods. The goal is not to list every ruler, dynasty, or regime, but to identify periods where the structure of society, state power, economy, religion, technology, or international order changed in a historically meaningful way.

## Reusable Prompt

Divide the history of a region, civilization, empire, or country into meaningful historical periods. Use nested lists. Give approximate dates. For each period, explain the core transition, main political or social pattern, and historical significance. Prefer structural transitions over pure ruler lists. When dates are uncertain or labels are debated, say so. Avoid treating modern nation-states as timeless units. Include useful historical common knowledge and connect the region to wider world history when relevant.

## How To Use This Framework

- When asked to periodize a region, civilization, empire, or country:
  - Use nested lists.
  - Give approximate dates.
  - Explain why each boundary matters.
  - Prefer structural transitions over pure ruler lists.
  - Mention uncertainty when dates or labels are debated.
  - Avoid treating modern nation-states as timeless historical units.
  - Include useful historical meaning, common knowledge, and comparison with wider world history.

- Recommended output format:
  - Region or civilization name `[date range]`
    - Major period `[date range]`
      - Key transition:
      - Political pattern:
      - Social/economic pattern:
      - Cultural/religious meaning:
      - Historical significance:

## General Rules Of Periodization

- Periodization is interpretive.
  - Dates are not always exact borders.
  - Some periods overlap because political, social, and cultural changes do not happen at the same speed.

- Use dynasties carefully.
  - A dynasty matters when it reflects a broader change in state structure, ideology, economy, or regional order.
  - A dynasty list by itself is not enough.

- Use modern country names carefully.
  - "China", "India", "Iran", "France", or "Germany" are useful labels, but their ancient and medieval histories often covered different territories and identities.

- A good historical period boundary usually marks one of these changes:
  - Agricultural or urban revolution.
  - State formation.
  - Empire formation or imperial collapse.
  - Major religious transformation.
  - Trade-system transformation.
  - Nomadic conquest or frontier restructuring.
  - Colonial expansion or decolonization.
  - Industrialization.
  - Nationalism and mass politics.
  - World war, Cold War, or globalization.

## Global Macro-Periodization

- Human prehistory and early societies `[before c. 3500 BCE]`
  - Paleolithic and Neolithic worlds.
  - Historical significance:
    - Long-term human migration, domestication, agriculture, and settled communities created the conditions for cities and states.

- Bronze Age civilizations `[c. 3500-1200 BCE]`
  - Major centers:
    - Mesopotamia.
    - Egypt.
    - Indus Valley.
    - Early China.
    - Aegean world.
  - Historical significance:
    - Writing, cities, kingship, long-distance trade, bronze technology, and early bureaucratic states emerged.

- Iron Age and classical empires `[c. 1200 BCE-500 CE]`
  - Major examples:
    - Assyria, Persia, Greece, Rome.
    - Maurya and Gupta India.
    - Qin and Han China.
  - Historical significance:
    - Large territorial empires, universal religions, classical philosophies, codified law, and transregional trade routes became central.

- Late antique transformation `[c. 200-750]`
  - Major changes:
    - Roman imperial transformation and fragmentation.
    - Sasanian-Roman rivalry.
    - Spread of Christianity and Buddhism.
    - Rise of Islam.
  - Historical significance:
    - The ancient imperial world gave way to new religious and political orders across Eurasia and North Africa.

- Post-classical and medieval regional systems `[c. 750-1500]`
  - Major patterns:
    - Islamic caliphates and sultanates.
    - Tang-Song-Yuan-Ming China.
    - Byzantine and Latin Christendom.
    - Indian Ocean trade.
    - Steppe empires and Mongol integration.
  - Historical significance:
    - Regional civilizations matured, while Afro-Eurasian exchange intensified through trade, migration, war, and religion.

- Early modern world `[c. 1450-1750]`
  - Major changes:
    - Oceanic empires.
    - Columbian exchange.
    - Gunpowder empires.
    - Reformation and state centralization.
  - Historical significance:
    - The world became more tightly connected through Atlantic, Indian Ocean, and Pacific networks.

- Industrial and imperial modernity `[c. 1750-1914]`
  - Major changes:
    - Industrial Revolution.
    - Atlantic revolutions.
    - European imperial expansion.
    - Modern capitalism and nationalism.
  - Historical significance:
    - Industrial power changed the global balance, producing modern states, mass politics, and colonial hierarchies.

- World wars and ideological conflict `[1914-1945]`
  - Major changes:
    - World War I.
    - Russian Revolution.
    - Great Depression.
    - Fascism, communism, liberal democracy.
    - World War II.
  - Historical significance:
    - Old empires collapsed and modern ideological mass politics reshaped the international order.

- Cold War and decolonization `[1945-1991]`
  - Major changes:
    - US-Soviet bipolarity.
    - Decolonization in Asia and Africa.
    - Nuclear politics.
    - Developmental states and non-alignment.
  - Historical significance:
    - Formal empires declined, but global power was reorganized through blocs, alliances, aid, revolutions, and proxy wars.

- Globalization and contemporary world `[1991-present]`
  - Major changes:
    - Post-Cold War order.
    - Global supply chains.
    - Digital revolution.
    - Rise of China.
    - Climate crisis and renewed great-power competition.
  - Historical significance:
    - Economic integration and geopolitical fragmentation coexist.

## Applied Regional Periodization

### Ancient Near East And Mesopotamia `[c. 3500-539 BCE]`

- Urban revolution and Sumerian city-states `[c. 3500-2000 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Cities, writing, temples, and kingship emerged in southern Mesopotamia.
  - Historical significance:
    - One of the earliest centers of urban civilization and written administration.

- Akkadian and post-Sumerian imperial experiments `[c. 2334-1800 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - The Akkadian Empire created an early model of territorial empire.
  - Historical significance:
    - Shows the shift from city-state politics to imperial rule.

- Old Babylonian and regional kingdoms `[c. 2000-1595 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Amorite dynasties and Babylonian law tradition.
  - Historical significance:
    - Hammurabi became a symbol of royal lawgiving.

- Late Bronze Age international system `[c. 1600-1200 BCE]`
  - Major powers:
    - Egypt, Hittites, Mitanni, Kassite Babylonia, Assyria.
  - Historical significance:
    - Diplomatic letters, royal marriage, chariot warfare, and palace economies linked the eastern Mediterranean and Near East.

- Iron Age empires `[c. 1200-539 BCE]`
  - Major powers:
    - Neo-Assyrian Empire `[911-609 BCE]`.
    - Neo-Babylonian Empire `[626-539 BCE]`.
  - Historical significance:
    - Mass deportation, imperial administration, siege warfare, and large-scale territorial control shaped later empires.

### Egypt `[c. 3100 BCE-642 CE]`

- Predynastic and Early Dynastic Egypt `[c. 4000-2686 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Nile communities consolidated into a unified kingdom.
  - Historical significance:
    - Egyptian kingship, writing, and state ritual took shape.

- Old Kingdom `[c. 2686-2181 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Pyramid-building monarchy and centralized royal authority.
  - Historical significance:
    - The pyramids express the power of divine kingship and state mobilization.

- First Intermediate Period `[c. 2181-2055 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Central authority weakened and local powers rose.
  - Historical significance:
    - A useful reminder that Egyptian history alternates between centralization and fragmentation.

- Middle Kingdom `[c. 2055-1650 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Reunification and administrative recovery.
  - Historical significance:
    - Often treated as a classical age of Egyptian literature and statecraft.

- Second Intermediate Period and Hyksos rule `[c. 1650-1550 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Foreign-ruled delta powers and new military technologies.
  - Historical significance:
    - Helped prepare the militarized expansion of the New Kingdom.

- New Kingdom `[c. 1550-1070 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Egypt became an imperial power in Nubia and the Levant.
  - Historical significance:
    - Age of Hatshepsut, Akhenaten, Tutankhamun, and Ramesses II.

- Late Period and foreign rule `[c. 1070-332 BCE]`
  - Major powers:
    - Libyan, Nubian, Assyrian, Persian, and native dynasties.
  - Historical significance:
    - Egypt remained prestigious but was increasingly embedded in wider imperial systems.

- Ptolemaic and Roman Egypt `[332 BCE-642 CE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Greek and then Roman rule over Egypt.
  - Historical significance:
    - Alexandria became a major center of Hellenistic science, learning, and Mediterranean trade.

### Iran And The Persianate World `[c. 3000 BCE-present]`

- Elamite and pre-imperial Iran `[c. 3000-550 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Iranian plateau cultures interacted with Mesopotamia.
  - Historical significance:
    - Elam and Media prepared the ground for Persian imperial formation.

- Achaemenid Empire `[550-330 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Cyrus, Darius, and Xerxes built a vast multiethnic empire.
  - Historical significance:
    - Satrapies, royal roads, imperial tolerance, and court culture influenced later empires.

- Hellenistic and Parthian periods `[330 BCE-224 CE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Alexander's conquest was followed by Seleucid and Parthian rule.
  - Historical significance:
    - Iranian, Greek, and steppe traditions mixed across the Silk Road world.

- Sasanian Empire `[224-651]`
  - Key transition:
    - Centralized Zoroastrian monarchy and long rivalry with Rome/Byzantium.
  - Historical significance:
    - One of late antiquity's great empires; its fall opened Iran to Islamic rule.

- Islamic conquest and caliphal integration `[651-900s]`
  - Key transition:
    - Iran became part of the Islamic world.
  - Historical significance:
    - Persian language and administrative culture later reshaped Islamic civilization from within.

- Persianate Islamic dynasties `[900s-1501]`
  - Major powers:
    - Samanids, Ghaznavids, Seljuks, Khwarazmians, Ilkhanids, Timurids.
  - Historical significance:
    - Persianate culture spread from Anatolia and Central Asia to India.

- Safavid Iran `[1501-1736]`
  - Key transition:
    - Twelver Shi'a Islam became central to Iranian state identity.
  - Historical significance:
    - A major boundary between Iran and the Sunni Ottoman and Uzbek worlds.

- Qajar and Pahlavi eras `[1789-1979]`
  - Key transition:
    - Imperial pressure, constitutionalism, modernization, and nationalism.
  - Historical significance:
    - Iran struggled to modernize without becoming formally colonized.

- Islamic Republic `[1979-present]`
  - Key transition:
    - Revolution created a modern theocratic republican system.
  - Historical significance:
    - Central to Middle Eastern geopolitics, political Islam, and oil-era state power.

### India And South Asia `[c. 3300 BCE-present]`

- Indus Valley Civilization `[c. 3300-1300 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Urban civilization in the Indus and Ghaggar-Hakra regions.
  - Historical significance:
    - Known for planned cities, trade, craft production, and undeciphered script.

- Vedic and early Indo-Aryan period `[c. 1500-600 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Vedic ritual culture, Sanskrit traditions, and early social stratification developed.
  - Historical significance:
    - Foundation for later Hindu traditions and political ideas.

- Second urbanization and shramana movements `[c. 600-321 BCE]`
  - Major changes:
    - Mahajanapadas, cities, coinage, Buddhism, Jainism.
  - Historical significance:
    - Age of the Buddha, Mahavira, and new debates about ritual, ethics, and liberation.

- Maurya Empire `[321-185 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - First large subcontinental empire.
  - Historical significance:
    - Ashoka's Buddhism became a major transregional religious force.

- Regional kingdoms and trade networks `[c. 185 BCE-320 CE]`
  - Major powers:
    - Shungas, Satavahanas, Indo-Greeks, Kushans.
  - Historical significance:
    - India was tied to Central Asia, the Roman trade world, and Buddhist exchange routes.

- Gupta and classical Sanskrit age `[c. 320-550]`
  - Key transition:
    - Imperial consolidation in northern India.
  - Historical significance:
    - Often associated with classical Sanskrit literature, science, art, and Hindu temple culture.

- Early medieval regionalization `[c. 550-1200]`
  - Major powers:
    - Chalukyas, Pallavas, Palas, Rashtrakutas, Cholas.
  - Historical significance:
    - Regional temple cultures, devotional religion, and Indian Ocean trade expanded.

- Delhi Sultanate `[1206-1526]`
  - Key transition:
    - Turkic-Afghan Islamic dynasties ruled much of northern India.
  - Historical significance:
    - Persianate political culture and Indo-Islamic institutions became deeply rooted.

- Mughal and early modern South Asia `[1526-1707/1857]`
  - Key transition:
    - Mughal imperial integration, land revenue systems, and court culture.
  - Historical significance:
    - One of the great early modern gunpowder empires.

- Regional successor states and colonial expansion `[c. 1707-1858]`
  - Major powers:
    - Marathas, Mysore, Sikhs, Nawabs, British East India Company.
  - Historical significance:
    - Commercial empire gradually became territorial colonial rule.

- British Raj and nationalism `[1858-1947]`
  - Key transition:
    - Direct British imperial rule after the 1857 uprising.
  - Historical significance:
    - Modern bureaucracy, railways, census politics, nationalism, and communal politics intensified.

- Independent South Asia `[1947-present]`
  - Key transition:
    - Partition created India and Pakistan; Bangladesh emerged in 1971.
  - Historical significance:
    - Decolonization, democracy, development, and nuclear geopolitics define the modern region.

### China `[c. 2000 BCE-present]`

- Early state formation `[c. 2000-771 BCE]`
  - Major periods:
    - Xia tradition `[c. 2070-1600 BCE]`.
    - Shang `[c. 1600-1046 BCE]`.
    - Western Zhou `[c. 1046-771 BCE]`.
  - Historical significance:
    - Bronze ritual culture, oracle bones, lineage aristocracy, and the Mandate of Heaven tradition emerged.

- Eastern Zhou transformation `[770-221 BCE]`
  - Subperiods:
    - Spring and Autumn `[770-476 BCE]`.
    - Warring States `[475-221 BCE]`.
  - Historical significance:
    - Aristocratic order collapsed; territorial bureaucratic states and major philosophical schools emerged.

- Early imperial unification `[221 BCE-220 CE]`
  - Major periods:
    - Qin `[221-207 BCE]`.
    - Han `[202 BCE-220 CE]`.
  - Historical significance:
    - The imperial model of emperor, bureaucracy, law, commanderies, Confucian learning, and frontier expansion was established.

- Division and northern-southern transformation `[220-589]`
  - Major periods:
    - Three Kingdoms `[220-280]`.
    - Jin and Sixteen Kingdoms `[266-439]`.
    - Northern and Southern Dynasties `[420-589]`.
  - Historical significance:
    - Political fragmentation, Buddhist expansion, aristocratic culture, and steppe-sedentary interaction reshaped China.

- Sui-Tang imperial reconstruction `[581-907]`
  - Major periods:
    - Sui `[581-618]`.
    - Tang `[618-907]`.
  - Historical significance:
    - Grand Canal, examination system, cosmopolitan empire, and East Asian cultural influence.

- Song and Inner Asian multi-state order `[907-1279]`
  - Major powers:
    - Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms `[907-979]`.
    - Liao `[916-1125]`.
    - Song `[960-1279]`.
    - Western Xia `[1038-1227]`.
    - Jin `[1115-1234]`.
  - Historical significance:
    - Commercialization, printing, Neo-Confucianism, urban culture, and coexistence with powerful northern regimes.

- Yuan-Ming-Qing imperial order `[1271-1912]`
  - Major periods:
    - Yuan `[1271-1368]`.
    - Ming `[1368-1644]`.
    - Qing `[1636/1644-1912]`.
  - Historical significance:
    - Mongol conquest, restoration of Han Chinese rule, then Manchu multiethnic empire; China became deeply tied to global silver and imperial competition.

- Republican and revolutionary China `[1912-1949]`
  - Major changes:
    - Republic of China, warlordism, Nationalist state, Japanese invasion, civil war.
  - Historical significance:
    - Monarchy ended; nationalism, socialism, and modern state-building competed.

- People's Republic of China `[1949-present]`
  - Major phases:
    - Mao era `[1949-1976]`.
    - Reform and opening `[1978-2012]`.
    - New era of centralized party-state and great-power competition `[2012-present]`.
  - Historical significance:
    - Revolution, planned economy, market reform, rapid industrialization, and global power status.

### Greece And The Hellenistic World `[c. 3000-30 BCE]`

- Aegean Bronze Age `[c. 3000-1100 BCE]`
  - Major cultures:
    - Minoan Crete `[c. 2000-1450 BCE]`.
    - Mycenaean Greece `[c. 1600-1100 BCE]`.
  - Historical significance:
    - Palace economies, Linear scripts, and heroic traditions later remembered in Greek myth.

- Greek Dark Age `[c. 1100-800 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Collapse of palace systems and smaller local communities.
  - Historical significance:
    - Background to Homeric epic and later polis formation.

- Archaic Greece `[c. 800-500 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Polis, colonization, alphabetic writing, hoplite warfare.
  - Historical significance:
    - Foundations of Greek political experimentation and Mediterranean expansion.

- Classical Greece `[c. 500-323 BCE]`
  - Major events:
    - Persian Wars, Athenian democracy, Peloponnesian War, Macedonian rise.
  - Historical significance:
    - Philosophy, drama, history-writing, democracy, and political theory became central to later Western traditions.

- Hellenistic world `[323-30 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Alexander's empire fragmented into Greek-ruled kingdoms.
  - Historical significance:
    - Greek language and culture spread across Egypt, the Near East, and Central Asia.

### Rome And Byzantium `[753 BCE-1453]`

- Roman Kingdom `[753-509 BCE]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Legendary and early institutional background of Rome.

- Roman Republic `[509-27 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Citizen army, Senate, law, and expansion across the Mediterranean.
  - Historical significance:
    - Republican institutions became a lasting model, even as expansion destabilized them.

- Roman Empire `[27 BCE-284 CE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Augustus created monarchy under republican language.
  - Historical significance:
    - Pax Romana, Roman law, urbanization, roads, and Mediterranean integration.

- Late Roman Empire `[284-476 in the West; continuing in the East]`
  - Key transition:
    - Diocletian and Constantine restructured the empire; Christianity became dominant.
  - Historical significance:
    - Late antiquity links the classical world to medieval Christianity and Islam.

- Byzantine Empire / Eastern Roman Empire `[330-1453]`
  - Key transition:
    - Constantinople preserved Roman imperial continuity in the eastern Mediterranean.
  - Historical significance:
    - Greek Christian empire, Roman law, Orthodox Christianity, and long conflict with Persia, Arabs, Turks, and Latin powers.

### Islamic World And The Middle East `[610-present]`

- Pre-Islamic Arabia and late antique Near East `[before 610]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Arabia stood between Byzantine, Sasanian, Red Sea, and Indian Ocean networks.

- Prophetic and Rashidun period `[610-661]`
  - Key transition:
    - Islam emerged and Arab-Muslim conquests began.
  - Historical significance:
    - A new religious and political community rapidly became an imperial force.

- Umayyad Caliphate `[661-750]`
  - Key transition:
    - Arab imperial monarchy centered in Damascus.
  - Historical significance:
    - Expansion from Iberia to Central Asia.

- Abbasid Caliphate and Islamic golden age `[750-1258]`
  - Key transition:
    - Baghdad became a center of administration, scholarship, translation, and commerce.
  - Historical significance:
    - Islamic civilization became more cosmopolitan and Persianate.

- Regional sultanates and fragmentation `[900-1500]`
  - Major powers:
    - Fatimids, Buyids, Seljuks, Ayyubids, Mamluks, various emirates.
  - Historical significance:
    - Political unity weakened, but Islamic law, scholarship, trade, and urban culture spread.

- Mongol shock and post-Mongol reconstruction `[1200s-1500s]`
  - Historical significance:
    - The fall of Baghdad in 1258 symbolized a major rupture, but new Islamic empires later emerged.

- Ottoman, Safavid, and Mughal age `[c. 1500-1800]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Gunpowder empires linked religion, military reform, imperial bureaucracy, and early modern trade.

- Imperial pressure, reform, and nationalism `[c. 1800-1945]`
  - Major changes:
    - Ottoman reform, European intervention, constitutionalism, colonial mandates.
  - Historical significance:
    - Modern Middle Eastern states emerged from imperial competition and post-Ottoman settlement.

- Postcolonial and oil-era Middle East `[1945-present]`
  - Major themes:
    - Arab nationalism, Israel-Palestine, oil politics, political Islam, US and Soviet/Russian involvement, Gulf monarchies.
  - Historical significance:
    - One of the central regions of modern energy, religion, migration, and geopolitics.

### Western Europe `[c. 500-present]`

- Post-Roman successor kingdoms `[c. 400-800]`
  - Major powers:
    - Franks, Visigoths, Ostrogoths, Lombards, Anglo-Saxons.
  - Historical significance:
    - Roman, Germanic, and Christian traditions fused into medieval Europe.

- Carolingian and early medieval order `[c. 750-1000]`
  - Key transition:
    - Charlemagne's empire and its division in 843.
  - Historical significance:
    - Foundations of later France, Germany, and medieval Latin Christendom.

- High medieval Europe `[c. 1000-1300]`
  - Major changes:
    - Agricultural growth, towns, universities, crusades, papal monarchy, feudal monarchies.
  - Historical significance:
    - Latin Christendom expanded politically, economically, and intellectually.

- Late medieval crisis and transformation `[c. 1300-1500]`
  - Major changes:
    - Black Death, Hundred Years' War, church crisis, stronger monarchies.
  - Historical significance:
    - Crisis weakened old structures and prepared Renaissance and Reformation change.

- Renaissance, Reformation, and state formation `[c. 1450-1650]`
  - Major changes:
    - Humanism, printing, Protestant Reformation, religious wars, oceanic expansion.
  - Historical significance:
    - European politics and religion were permanently reorganized.

- Absolutism, constitutionalism, and Atlantic empires `[c. 1650-1789]`
  - Historical significance:
    - France, Britain, Spain, the Dutch Republic, and others competed through war, trade, and colonies.

- Revolution, industry, and nationalism `[1789-1914]`
  - Major changes:
    - French Revolution, Napoleonic wars, Industrial Revolution, liberalism, socialism, nationalism.
  - Historical significance:
    - Modern mass politics and industrial power transformed Europe and the world.

- World wars and European crisis `[1914-1945]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Europe destroyed much of its old imperial order through two world wars.

- Cold War and European integration `[1945-1991]`
  - Major changes:
    - Western bloc, Eastern bloc, NATO, Warsaw Pact, European Communities.
  - Historical significance:
    - Europe was divided but also began integration after centuries of interstate war.

- European Union and post-Cold War Europe `[1991-present]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Integration, enlargement, migration, financial crisis, Brexit, and renewed security tensions define the period.

### France `[c. 50 BCE-present]`

- Roman Gaul `[c. 50 BCE-486]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Romanization shaped language, cities, law, and Christianity.

- Frankish and Carolingian world `[486-987]`
  - Historical significance:
    - The Frankish kingdom linked Roman Christian inheritance with Germanic kingship.

- Capetian and medieval monarchy `[987-1453]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Royal authority slowly expanded from a weak core around Paris.

- Early modern monarchy `[1453-1789]`
  - Major phases:
    - Renaissance monarchy.
    - Wars of Religion.
    - Bourbon absolutism.
  - Historical significance:
    - France became a centralized great power.

- Revolution and Napoleonic era `[1789-1815]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Citizenship, secular law, nationalism, and mass politics spread across Europe.

- Nineteenth-century regime experiments `[1815-1870]`
  - Major regimes:
    - Bourbon Restoration, July Monarchy, Second Republic, Second Empire.
  - Historical significance:
    - France repeatedly tested monarchy, republic, and empire.

- Republican France and world wars `[1870-1945]`
  - Historical significance:
    - The Third Republic survived crisis but was shattered by defeat in 1940.

- Fourth and Fifth Republics `[1946-present]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Decolonization, presidential republicanism, European integration, and secular national identity.

### Britain And The United Kingdom `[43 CE-present]`

- Roman Britain `[43-410]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Roman cities, roads, and frontier systems shaped parts of Britain but did not fully determine later English identity.

- Anglo-Saxon and Viking age `[c. 410-1066]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Germanic kingdoms, Christianization, and Viking pressure created the background of England.

- Norman and Angevin monarchy `[1066-1216]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Norman conquest transformed landholding, law, aristocracy, and ties to France.

- Medieval monarchy and Parliament `[1215-1485]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Magna Carta, Parliament, Hundred Years' War, and Wars of the Roses shaped political institutions.

- Tudor and Stuart transformation `[1485-1714]`
  - Major changes:
    - Reformation, union of crowns, civil war, execution of Charles I, Glorious Revolution.
  - Historical significance:
    - Parliamentary monarchy and Protestant state identity took shape.

- British Empire and industrialization `[1714-1914]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Britain became a leading naval, imperial, financial, and industrial power.

- World wars, welfare state, and decolonization `[1914-1979]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Britain won wars but lost imperial primacy; the welfare state expanded.

- Post-imperial Britain `[1979-present]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Neoliberal reform, devolution, EU membership and Brexit, and changing global role.

### Germany And Central Europe `[843-present]`

- East Frankish and Ottonian foundations `[843-962]`
  - Historical significance:
    - East Francia became the core of medieval German kingship.

- Holy Roman Empire `[962-1806]`
  - Historical significance:
    - A decentralized imperial order linking German, Italian, Burgundian, and Central European politics.

- Reformation and confessional division `[1517-1648]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Lutheran reform and religious war reshaped European Christianity and German politics.

- Prussia, Austria, and German dualism `[1648-1866]`
  - Historical significance:
    - German politics revolved around rivalry between Habsburg Austria and rising Prussia.

- German Empire `[1871-1918]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Unification under Prussia made Germany an industrial and military great power.

- Weimar Republic and Nazi Germany `[1918-1945]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Democratic crisis, fascism, genocide, and World War II made this one of modern history's central ruptures.

- Divided Germany `[1949-1990]`
  - Historical significance:
    - West and East Germany became front-line states of the Cold War.

- Reunified Germany `[1990-present]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Germany became central to the European Union and post-Cold War Europe.

### Russia And Eastern Europe `[c. 800-present]`

- Kievan Rus' `[c. 880-1240]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Eastern Slavic state formation and Orthodox Christianization.

- Mongol domination and regional principalities `[1240-1480]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Tribute politics and steppe power shaped Muscovy's rise.

- Muscovite state `[c. 1300-1547]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Moscow consolidated territory and claimed Orthodox imperial legitimacy.

- Tsardom of Russia `[1547-1721]`
  - Historical significance:

### Korea `[c. 1000 BCE-present]`

- Early Korea and Gojoseon `[c. 1000-108 BCE]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Bronze culture and early state traditions formed in the Korean peninsula and Manchurian borderlands.

- Proto-Three Kingdoms and Chinese commanderies `[108 BCE-300s CE]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Local polities developed while Han commanderies connected the region to Chinese imperial systems.

- Three Kingdoms `[57 BCE-668 CE]`
  - Major states:
    - Goguryeo, Baekje, Silla, Gaya confederacy.
  - Historical significance:
    - Buddhism, aristocratic states, warfare, and cultural exchange with China and Japan.

- Unified Silla and Balhae `[668-935]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Peninsula consolidation in the south and a northern Manchurian-Korean state in Balhae.

- Goryeo `[918-1392]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Buddhism, aristocratic culture, celadon, movable metal type, and Mongol influence.

- Joseon `[1392-1897]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Neo-Confucian bureaucracy, Hangul, yangban society, and tributary relations with Ming/Qing China.

- Empire, colonization, and division `[1897-1948]`
  - Major changes:
    - Korean Empire, Japanese annexation `[1910-1945]`, liberation, US-Soviet occupation.
  - Historical significance:
    - Modern nationalism formed under imperial pressure and colonial rule.

- North and South Korea `[1948-present]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Korean War, Cold War division, South Korean industrial democracy, North Korean hereditary socialist state.

### Japan `[c. 14000 BCE-present]`

- Jomon and Yayoi worlds `[c. 14000 BCE-250 CE]`
  - Key transition:
    - From hunter-gatherer Jomon culture to Yayoi rice agriculture, metallurgy, and social hierarchy.
  - Historical significance:
    - Foundation for later state formation in the archipelago.

- Kofun and Asuka state formation `[c. 250-710]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Yamato rule, tomb culture, Buddhism, Chinese-style institutions.

- Nara and Heian court civilization `[710-1185]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Imperial court culture, classical literature, Buddhism, and aristocratic rule.

- Warrior governments and medieval Japan `[1185-1603]`
  - Major periods:
    - Kamakura `[1185-1333]`.
    - Muromachi `[1336-1573]`.
    - Sengoku `[c. 1467-1600]`.
  - Historical significance:
    - Samurai rule, shogunate institutions, Zen culture, and prolonged civil war.

- Tokugawa / Edo Japan `[1603-1868]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Peace, urban culture, status order, domain system, controlled foreign relations.

- Meiji and imperial Japan `[1868-1945]`
  - Major periods:
    - Meiji `[1868-1912]`.
    - Taisho `[1912-1926]`.
    - Showa imperial period `[1926-1945]`.
  - Historical significance:
    - Rapid modernization, constitutional monarchy, industrialization, empire, and war.

- Postwar Japan `[1945-present]`
  - Major periods:
    - Occupation `[1945-1952]`.
    - High-growth era `[1950s-1973]`.
    - Heisei stagnation and reform `[1989-2019]`.
    - Reiwa `[2019-present]`.
  - Historical significance:
    - Pacifist constitution, US alliance, economic miracle, aging society, and technological capitalism.

- Chinese domination and local resistance `[111 BCE-938 CE]`
  - Major phases:
    - Han to Tang imperial rule with periods of revolt and autonomy.

- Revolution, partition, and war `[1945-1975]`

  - Historical significance:
    - Vietnam became a central Cold War battlefield.

- Socialist Republic and reform `[1976-present]`
  - Major changes:
    - Reunification, postwar hardship, Doi Moi reforms from 1986.
  - Historical significance:
    - Market socialism and regional integration transformed Vietnam's economy.

### The Americas `[c. 15000 BCE-present]`

- Peopling and early societies `[before c. 2000 BCE]`
  - Historical significance:
    - Human migration, adaptation, and agriculture created diverse societies across the hemisphere.

- Mesoamerican civilizations `[c. 2000 BCE-1521 CE]`
  - Major cultures:
    - Olmec, Maya, Teotihuacan, Toltec, Mexica/Aztec.
  - Historical significance:
    - Cities, calendars, writing, ritual kingship, and tribute empires developed independently.

- Andean civilizations `[c. 3000 BCE-1532 CE]`
  - Major cultures:
    - Norte Chico, Chavin, Moche, Wari, Tiwanaku, Inca.
  - Historical significance:
    - Highland-coastal integration, road systems, state labor, and imperial rule without alphabetic writing.

- North American regional societies `[c. 1000 BCE-1600 CE]`
  - Major cultures:
    - Adena, Hopewell, Mississippian, Puebloan, Iroquoian and many others.
  - Historical significance:
    - Complex societies developed through agriculture, trade, mound-building, and confederacies.

- European conquest and colonial Americas `[1492-1800s]`
  - Major powers:
    - Spain, Portugal, Britain, France, Netherlands.
  - Historical significance:
    - Columbian exchange, disease, slavery, plantation economies, and settler colonialism transformed the world.

- Atlantic revolutions and independent states `[1776-1830s]`
  - Major events:
    - American Revolution, Haitian Revolution, Spanish American independence, Brazilian independence.
  - Historical significance:
    - Ideas of citizenship and sovereignty spread, but slavery and inequality persisted.

- Nation-building, expansion, and dependency `[1800s-1945]`
  - Historical significance:
    - US expansion, Latin American republics, abolition, export economies, and foreign intervention shaped the hemisphere.

- Cold War and contemporary Americas `[1945-present]`
  - Major themes:
    - US superpower role, Cuban Revolution, military dictatorships, democratization, migration, neoliberal reform, indigenous movements.
  - Historical significance:
    - The Americas became central to global capitalism, ideology, migration, and cultural production.

### Oceania And The Pacific `[c. 50000 BCE-present]`

- First settlement of Sahul `[c. 50000 BCE-3000 BCE]`
  - Main regions:
    - Australia, New Guinea, Tasmania.
  - Historical significance:
    - Aboriginal Australian and Papuan societies represent some of the oldest continuous human cultural traditions.
  - Useful common knowledge:
    - "Sahul" refers to the Ice Age landmass that connected Australia, New Guinea, and Tasmania when sea levels were lower.

- Austronesian expansion and Lapita culture `[c. 3000-500 BCE]`
  - Key transition:
    - Seafaring communities moved through Island Southeast Asia into the Pacific.
  - Historical significance:
    - Canoe technology, navigation, agriculture, and pottery enabled one of history's great maritime migrations.

- Polynesian settlement and oceanic chiefdoms `[c. 1000 BCE-1500 CE]`
  - Major regions:
    - Samoa, Tonga, Tahiti, Hawaii, Rapa Nui, Aotearoa/New Zealand.
  - Historical significance:
    - Long-distance navigation connected islands across an enormous oceanic world.
  - Useful common knowledge:
    - Polynesian history is often underestimated because it was not organized around large continental empires, but its navigational achievement was exceptional.

- European contact and colonial Pacific `[c. 1500-1945]`
  - Major changes:
    - Spanish, Dutch, British, French, German, American, and Japanese expansion.
    - Missionary activity, disease, plantation economies, settler colonies, and military bases.
  - Historical significance:
    - The Pacific became part of global imperial competition and maritime trade.

- Australia and New Zealand settler societies `[1788-present]`
  - Key transition:
    - British colonization produced settler states over Indigenous lands.
  - Historical significance:
    - Modern Australia and New Zealand must be understood through both settler democracy and Indigenous dispossession.

- Pacific War and decolonization `[1941-present]`
  - Major changes:
    - Japanese expansion, US island campaigns, nuclear testing, postwar trusteeships, and independence movements.
  - Historical significance:
    - The Pacific became central to World War II, Cold War strategy, and later climate politics.

- Contemporary Pacific `[1991-present]`
  - Major themes:
    - Climate change, sea-level rise, migration, fisheries, tourism, resource extraction, and US-China strategic competition.
  - Historical significance:
    - Small island states have become important voices in climate diplomacy and maritime sovereignty.
